01 Context

Airtable just changed hands.

On August 4, Bending Spoons acquired Airtable for $1.285 billion. Overnight, 500,000+ organizations, including 80% of the Fortune 100, have their critical work sitting on a platform under new ownership.

  • The acquirer's pattern is known. Bending Spoons (Evernote, WeTransfer, Meetup, AOL, Eventbrite) buys software and monetizes hard. Airtable customers are reading the same headlines.
  • Uncertainty creates openness. Roadmap, pricing, and support are now question marks. Customers are open to a conversation they weren't having a month ago.
  • The window is now. Every competitor sees the same opening. Speed and specificity win the accounts.
Validate before the session: confirm the acquisition figures and any customer-facing framing with comms/legal. Keep the acquirer references factual and on public record.

05 How to open

Start the conversation.

One approved opener per segment, on the campaign's line: a home whose roadmap is built around your work. Warm, not opportunistic. Consultative, not a pounce.

For · Lost deals
"Worth a fresh look?"

Open on the change, not the sell. "A lot has shifted for Airtable since we last spoke. If the roadmap is a question for your team, it may be worth another look at how monday fits now."

For · Dual usage
"One platform, less to manage."

Lead with consolidation value. "You already run part of your work on monday. With Airtable's future in flux, here's what moving the rest onto one platform looks like, and how simple the switch is."

For · New footprint
"A home built around your work."

Introduce the alternative. "If your team is weighing what Airtable's acquisition means, monday is a platform built for the long run, where people and agents run the work together. Happy to show you."

Legal sign-off required before any outreach. Every line here is a draft. Legal must clear the acquisition references and competitive claims. Do not send until approved.
