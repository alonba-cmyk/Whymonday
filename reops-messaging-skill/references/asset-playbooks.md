# Asset Playbooks

Per-artifact templates. Each reuses the same architecture and formulas, tuned to the format. Confirm which artifact you're building, then follow its skeleton.

## Marketing / product email

**Shape:** hook → named villain → the collapse to "just describe it" → three concrete examples → generous CTA.
- **Subject line:** give options by angle, recommend one (see process-and-craft). A/B two hooks over one body is native to how Alon works.
- **Body:** two sentences max per paragraph. Name the villain ("die waiting on developers"), then the release ("builds them in minutes, no code, no dev ticket").
- **Examples line:** a triad of capitalized, specific tools ("A Campaign Health Dashboard? An Asset Request Portal? An Event Command Center?").
- **Close:** warm and low-friction ("build what you need, on us"). End on a temporal or "just describe it" payoff.

## Persona landing page (per department)

**Reuse one skeleton, re-tune copy per audience's deepest desire.**
1. Hero: `turn X into Y` ("Marketing AI agents that turn strategy into launch-ready campaigns").
2. Division of labor: `You [judgment]. Agents [execution].`
3. Agent showcase: characters with names + one-line temporal-payoff outcomes.
4. `Every touchpoint. One platform.` antithesis section.
5. Build tools: "Build the [dept] tools you've always wanted."
6. Run your stack on one workspace.
7. Customer Zero: "See how our [dept] teams do it."
8. Proof: `Recognized by analysts, loved by users.`
9. Enterprise trust pillars: Transparency / Accountability / Control / Security, one line each.

## Narrative pitch / story deck

**Arc:** Vision → Problem (capacity) → Diagnosis (4-part skeleton) → Solution (reframe + Workspace·Context·Control) → Proof (testimonials, 5,117 agents, Monday-on-Monday) → CTA.
- Open on vision, not pain. Hero is the controlling idea, broken across lines.
- Persona vignettes to make the problem concrete ("Different teams. Same feeling.").
- Every slide: eyebrow (category) → headline (one claim) → subhead (the proof) → bullets. Eyebrow never repeats the headline.
- **Build variants by swapping the front, not the spine.** Keep the back half fixed; change the opening frame per audience.

## GTM vision / positioning document

**Shape:** WHAT/WHY/HOW matrix up front, then prose per row.
- One controlling tension for the whole doc ("ambition vs. execution").
- Heritage → "Now" cadence to earn the pivot.
- Challenge → Opportunity table, each line paired with an analyst source.
- Contrastive stats ("X, yet Y").
- Progression scaffolds (timeline, stages, day-in-the-life) to make the shift feel inevitable.
- Close with the `it was never about X` move.

## Session titles (events like Elevate)

Use `hook : descriptor`, one per audience, bent to that department's appetite. Same offering, different hook.
- IT (wants to lead responsibly): "IT leading the AI charge: Driving responsible AI adoption across the organization."
- PMO & Ops (wants delivery): "Every project, delivered: What execution looks like when agents handle operations."
- Enterprise (wants scale): "From personal productivity to business impact: How enterprises drive AI transformation."
- Marketing (wants volume): "Demand without limits: How marketing teams execute more campaigns."

## In-product / onboarding modal

Short, warm, action-first. `just describe it` + temporal payoff + one-verb CTA.
- "Your first agent is just a prompt away."
- "Put one of our agents to work, or describe what you're looking for and we'll build a custom one. Either way, the job's done in minutes."
- CTA: "Let's go."
- Show agent characters (name + role + live status).

## First-person narrative (Customer Zero / LinkedIn / interview)

See process-and-craft § "First-person voice." Concrete sensory detail, exact names, close to the reader, no motivational-quote openers.
