# Channel Calibration

This layer sits **above** the asset playbooks. Before choosing a template, calibrate the voice to the channel. The spine never moves; the front flexes.

## What stays constant in every channel (the spine)

People and agents together. `You [judgment]. Agents [execution]`. Workspace, Context, Control. Specificity over superlative. Outcome-led. No em-dashes. Agents multiply people, never diminish them. This is the DNA and it does not change by channel.

## What flexes by channel (the front)

| Channel | POV | Sentence | Proof | Villain | CTA | Signature device |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Email** | You (warm, personal) | 2 sentences/paragraph, very short | Almost none | Concrete, named (dev ticket, the close) | Explicit action ("Claim your free trial") | Named villain + "just describe it" |
| **In-product modal** | You / We ("we'll build") | Ultra-compact | None | Soft | One verb ("Let's go") | Temporal payoff |
| **Landing page** | You | Medium paragraphs, antithesis headlines | Analysts + users | Muted | "Get started" | `Every X. One Y.` |
| **Sales / story deck** | You (the audience) | Fragments, broken lines | Testimonials, 5,117, Monday-on-Monday | Framed as capacity | "See it in action" | Persona vignette + 4-part diagnosis |
| **Session title** | Neutral | Hook + descriptor | None | Implied | None (the title is the hook) | `hook : descriptor` |
| **Vision doc (internal)** | We / Our (rallies the org) | Full prose, tables | Cited sources (HBR, Gartner, MIT) | Systemic, source-anchored | None (it's a "Bottom line") | WHAT/WHY/HOW, scaffolds, "it was never about X" |
| **Funnel / RevOps readout (internal)** | We | Terse, analytical | Raw data | The gap (e.g. demand with no funnel) | "Next step" | `//` insight comments |
| **Customer Zero / LinkedIn** | I (first person, narrative) | Sensory, concrete | The story itself | The personal frustration | Speak directly to the reader | Concrete detail over abstraction |

## Two distinctions that organize everything

### 1. Internal vs. external (the strongest axis)
- **Internal** (Vision doc, funnel readout): POV is **"We."** Analytical, source-cited, no CTA. The job is to align the organization.
- **External** (email, landing, deck, modal, session title): POV is **"You."** Outcome-led, CTA-driven. The job is to move a customer.

If unsure which a request is, ask. It changes POV, proof, and whether there's a CTA at all.

### 2. Push vs. pull
- **Push** (email, modal): interruptive. The first line must earn attention; brevity is survival; the hook carries the weight. This is where the em-dash ban matters most, because an em-dash breaks the personal, human feel and reads as AI.
- **Pull** (landing page, deck): the reader came to you. You can build a longer arc and layer in more proof.

## One device that swaps by register

Customer-facing channels land on a **temporal payoff** ("ready the morning after"). Strategic prose swaps it for **`From X to Y`** and **progression scaffolds**, because there the move is transformation, not speed. Don't put "in minutes" in a vision doc, and don't put a five-stage evolution scaffold in a win-back email.

## Quick calibration checklist

Before writing, answer:
1. Internal or external? → sets POV (We vs. You) and whether there's a CTA.
2. Push or pull? → sets how hard the first line works and how short to go.
3. Which audience's deepest desire? → sets the front (keep the spine).
4. Does this channel want speed (temporal payoff) or transformation (From X to Y)?
