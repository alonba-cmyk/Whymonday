# Voice & Tone

Sentence-level mechanics. How the copy sounds once the architecture is set.

## The register

Confident, warm, plain. First-person plural and present tense for the company voice. No jargon. Human warmth shows up in small casual generosities ("on us," "there's a story," "we'll build it for you"). Confident, never boastful: state what's true and let it stand, no superlatives that puff up monday or put down anyone else.

## Rhythm

- **Fragments are tools.** Verbless sentences are welcome when they hit harder. "More requests. More priorities. Same hours."
- **Triads.** Group in threes: "plan, execute, and deliver," "understands, connects, and runs your work."
- **Periods as beats.** Break rhythm with periods, not em-dashes. "24/7. Any Language. Any Timezone."
- **Broken-line headlines.** Let line breaks do the pacing (see copy-formulas).
- **Heritage → Now.** Parallel past-tense triad, then a "Now" turn.

## Punctuation rules

- **No em-dashes in final copy. Any asset. No exceptions.** Replace with a period, comma, colon, or a restructure. This overrides older shipped work that used them.
- Colons are fine and encouraged for the `hook : descriptor` title shape.
- Question marks are fine for `What if...?` openers.

## Word choices

| Prefer | Over | Why |
|---|---|---|
| People | Humans | The throughline is "people and agents." Use "humans" only to stress the human's exclusive role ("humans in the loop"). |
| You / your team | We / us / our company | Reframe around the reader. |
| Outcomes, results | Features, capabilities | Land on the payoff. |
| Specific numbers | "thousands," "many," "leading" | Specificity is the credibility. |
| Named villains | "inefficiency," "friction" | Concrete enemies (the dev ticket, the wait). |

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Open on the vision or the reader's world | Open on what monday did, or on the problem |
| Open with a truth, a `What if...?`, or a hook | Open with a negation ("We don't...", "It's not...") |
| Use negation as a mid-pivot or closer | Bury the negation's payoff |
| Keep two sentences max per email paragraph | Write dense blocks |
| Say one new thing per section | Restate "people + agents" or "do more with less" repeatedly |
| End on the human/business outcome | End on the mechanism |
| Flatter the reader ("running at 100%") | Imply the reader is behind or deficient |
| Cite a source next to a stat | Drop bare claims |
| Cut anything that doesn't serve the one idea | Keep a nice line that wanders off-message |

## The eyebrow rule

In any block with an eyebrow/kicker above a headline, the eyebrow must not restate the headline. It names the category; the headline makes the claim. (Alon has fixed this exact issue in shipped decks: "replace the eyebrow to stop repeating the H2.")
